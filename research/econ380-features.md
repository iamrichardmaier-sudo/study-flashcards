# ECON 380 (Intermediate Micro Theory 1) — Interactive Feature Spec

Scope: consumer theory (preferences, utility max, Lagrangians, Slutsky), producer theory (production, costs), partial equilibrium (perfect competition, monopoly, basic game theory). Model playgrounds ranked by study value, designed to complement the flashcards/audio/practice-test base app.

## 1. Consumer Optimizer + Slutsky Decomposition Playground
Interactive indifference map with sliders for prices, income, and utility-function parameters; shows the tangency optimum live and animates the substitution vs. income effect split when a price changes. This is the mathematical heart of the course and the hardest to internalize from static graphs.

## 2. Cost Curve Lab (Isoquant → Isocost → MC/AC)
Build a Cobb–Douglas/CES production function with input-price sliders; app derives the expansion path, cost-minimizing input mix, and the MC/AC/AVC curves. Bridges the gap between production theory and the supply curve students must draw on exams.

## 3. Market Shifter Playground with Tax & Deadweight Loss
Supply/demand with draggable shifters, live consumer/producer surplus triangles, and widgets for per-unit taxes, subsidies, price floors/ceilings showing the wedge and deadweight-loss geometry. Also hosts an elasticity calculator linked to tax-incidence intuition.

## 4. Monopoly vs. Perfect Competition Comparator
Side-by-side MR/MC profit-maximization visualizer with profit rectangles, deadweight loss, and the Lerner markup rule; toggle between market structures and explore first/second/third-degree price discrimination. Turns comparative statics into an intuition drill.

## 5. Edgeworth Box Explorer
Interactive lens with draggable endowments, contract curve tracing, and the competitive-equilibrium tangency. The geometry students consistently misdraw, made checkable in seconds.

## 6. Game Theory / Cournot Nash Solver
Enter a 2×2 payoff matrix to highlight best responses, dominant strategies, and Nash equilibria; extend to Cournot duopoly reaction functions converging to equilibrium. Covers the typical end-of-course oligopoly unit.

Notes: features build on each other (1→2→3→4 is the course arc). All graphs should allow one-tap "quiz me" mode hiding labels, and export a snapshot for flashcard generation. Deeper general-equilibrium, externalities, and asymmetric-information topics belong to ECON 382 — keep this app to the canonical 380 core.
