# IHUM 242 (Islamic Humanities) — Interactive Feature Spec

**Instructors:** Kevin Blankinship (Arabic literature, Islamic civilization, Qur'an; medieval poetry specialist) & Robert Colson (interdisciplinary humanities; modern/postcolonial literature, nationalism).

**Course shape:** classical Islamic civilization (origins, caliphates, Golden Age philosophy/science/poetry, art & architecture, Ottoman/Safavid/Mughal empires) plus modern/postcolonial Islamic world (colonialism, nationalism, contemporary literature).

## Top features (ordered by value)

1. **Interactive map game** — Pin empires at their peak (Umayyad, Abbasid, Ottoman, Safavid, Mughal), key cities (Mecca, Medina, Baghdad, Córdoba, Cairo, Istanbul, Isfahan, Delhi), trade routes, and battle sites; expansion-timeline slider (622–750+). *Justification: geography is the hardest rote-memorization load in humanities survey courses, and a game makes it stick.*

2. **Timeline explorer** — Scrollable timeline from 570 CE through the caliphates, Golden Age figures, Mongol invasions, the gunpowder empires, colonialism, to the modern era; click any event/figure for a summary. *Justification: exams test sequencing and cause-effect (e.g., Abbasid collapse → Mamluk/Ottoman rise); visual chronology encodes this far better than lists.*

3. **Key-figures matcher** — Match philosophers, poets, and scientists to works and ideas: Ibn Sina (Canon of Medicine), Ibn Rushd (Aristotle commentaries), al-Ghazali (Incoherence of the Philosophers), Rumi (Masnavi), Ibn Khaldun (Muqaddima), al-Ma'arri, Ibn Battuta. *Justification: name–work–idea triples are the core unit of humanities assessment and are painful to drill with plain flashcards.*

4. **Art & architecture gallery quiz** — Identify buildings (Dome of the Rock, Alhambra, Blue Mosque, Taj Mahal, Sheikh Lotfollah) and match features (muqarnas, arabesque, minaret styles, dome types) to dynasties/regions. *Justification: visual identification is a classic humanities exam format that text flashcards cannot serve.*

5. **Primary-source excerpt reader** — Short excerpts (Qur'an, hadith, Ibn Khaldun, Rumi, Ibn Battuta's travels) with guided reading questions and key vocabulary. *Justification: both instructors are literature scholars — close reading of texts is the skill exams actually test.*

6. **Concepts-in-context drills** — Scenario-based questions rather than definitions: e.g., "A merchant endows a school in 11th-century Baghdad — which institution?" (waqf/madrasa). Terms: umma, sharia, fiqh, Sufism, madrasa, waqf, caliphate. *Justification: exams test concepts applied in context, not isolated definitions.*
